## A site that reacts

My friend Allie creates real-time, audio-reactive lighting for events. She wanted
a site that responded like her shows.

I built a background that reacts to the video and the cursor, with splashes on
click. Allie chose the typeface and shared sites she liked; those references gave
us a starting point.

## Moving through the work

This was my first experiment with Three.js. I wanted the whole site to feel
three-dimensional, including a slight parallax in the gallery. Inverted previews
reveal their colour on hover.

The viewer is the conductor: moving the cursor, clicking the background, and
choosing a clip all change the scene. I wanted that navigation to feel intuitive
and satisfying.

## The projector room

Opening a clip takes you into a room built around a projected image. Bloom lets
the light spill beyond the screen.

The supplied footage was heavily compressed. I used animated grain to help mask
the artifacts and give the projection its texture.

## Keeping video light

An automated encoding pipeline prepares smaller playback versions while preserving
the masters. Previews load around the visible part of the gallery and play on
hover; video starts automatically where the browser permits it.

Three.js, through React Three Fiber, gives the gallery and projector room real
depth and camera movement. React connects the scenes to navigation and playback
state; Next.js keeps the public pages and admin routes in one app. HLS.js switches
between compressed video renditions, keeping playback practical alongside the 3D
rendering.
