## The classification task and my contribution

The McGill Bot or Not challenge asks participants to identify bot accounts from
supplied social-media datasets without wrongly flagging human accounts. Each
account has a profile and a timeline of timestamped posts; the target is the
account, not whether an individual post sounds machine-generated. The output is a
file of account IDs flagged as bots.

This was a solo implementation. I built the feature extraction, model comparison,
threshold selection, reporting and prediction commands. English and French have
separate fitted models and thresholds. The bilingual command coordinates their
runs and collects their artifacts; it does not train a multilingual model.

## Combining account and text features

I represented each account through both numerical summaries and its post text:

- **Profile and activity:** post count, the supplied activity z-score, profile-field
  lengths, and flags for empty descriptions or locations.
- **Posting behaviour:** duplicate-post ratio, average URL and mention counts,
  variation in post lengths, intervals between posts, and posting-hour entropy—a
  measure of how spread out the account's activity is across the day.
- **Text:** posts joined into one document per account, then represented with
  TF-IDF. Sequences that recur within an account but are less common across
  training accounts receive more weight.

The comparison asks whether numerical behaviour summaries are enough, or whether
text patterns add a useful signal. Character sequences capture recurring fragments
without requiring whole-word matches; word sequences preserve phrases. These are
representation choices, not a claim that every feature improves performance.

The five model configurations make those alternatives explicit:

- **Logistic regression** joins character 3–5-gram TF-IDF with scaled numerical
  account features in one feature vector.
- **Hybrid text** combines word 1–2-gram and character 3–5-gram TF-IDF, each
  compressed with truncated SVD into a smaller dense vector, with an extended set
  of scaled account features. A calibrated logistic regression uses that combined
  vector. These are fitted TF-IDF/SVD representations, not pretrained
  language-model embeddings.
- **Histogram gradient boosting** uses the base numerical features to model
  nonlinear combinations of account behaviour.
- **ExtraTrees** uses extended numerical features, including vocabulary diversity,
  punctuation ratios, retweet share and burst-versus-gap timing statistics.
- **The ensemble** blends the calibrated bot probabilities from histogram gradient
  boosting and ExtraTrees. Its component weights are selected using out-of-fold
  predictions, allowing the comparison to test a blend of the two tree approaches.

The [saved April run](https://github.com/TheTexta/bot-or-not-mcgill/blob/5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4/runs/20260404T162558Z-both-official-first-optimize/summary.json)
selected the tree ensemble for both languages.

## Selecting models and thresholds

An official shard is one supplied, labelled dataset: account profiles and post
timelines associated with a collection window and topics. The official-first
workflow uses English shards 1, 3 and 5, and French shards 2, 4 and 6. It validates
on one whole shard at a time while fitting on the other two. This preserves
collection boundaries during selection rather than mixing accounts from every
collection into each fold. The aim is to favour models that work across
collections, not just within one.

Selection follows the challenge's utility score, `2TP − 6FP − 2FN`, rather than
maximising F1. A false accusation against a human costs three times the penalty
for missing a bot. Each candidate's out-of-fold probabilities are used to choose a
threshold; the candidate with the highest utility wins, with fewer false positives
preferred in a tie. Thresholds are selected separately for English and French so
each language's validation predictions determine its own operating point rather
than assuming a shared probability scale or cutoff.

**These out-of-fold predictions are selection data, not an independent final
test.** Model choice, ensemble weights and thresholds all use them. After selection,
the estimator is refit on the combined official training data for its language.
The workflow records configurations, selection reports, model bundles and
prediction artifacts. These make the choices inspectable and support future
reproduction; they do not establish that the historical numbers have been
reproduced.

### Training-command differences

The lower-level training command can reserve a stratified primary holdout before
model and threshold selection. The saved official-first run instead uses whole
official shards for selection and has no additional primary holdout
(`selected_holdout` is null). The pinned
[modeling tests](https://github.com/TheTexta/bot-or-not-mcgill/blob/5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4/tests/test_modeling.py)
check that reserved holdout accounts stay out of selection-time fitting and that
predefined official fold IDs are respected. Those tests verify split behaviour,
not numerical reproduction of the saved scores.

### Overlap audit method

The audit inspected checked-in inputs at revision
`5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4`. It compared SHA-256 fingerprints of
sorted complete post-text lists, ignoring post order but preserving repeated
occurrences. It did not normalise case or whitespace. A separate fingerprint
compared canonical full profile/post payloads with account and post identifiers
excluded, retaining timestamps and other fields.

The 252 English timeline matches were against official shards 1, 3 and 5; none
were identical full records. The 164 French timeline matches were present against
official shard 2 alone; 131 also matched the ID-stripped full payload. The
historical reports name the same dataset IDs, but lack saved input checksums to
establish historical-byte parity. These checks identify exact content reuse in
the inspected files, not a reconstruction of the training run.

## Preventing overlap in future runs

Consider an account copied into a practice file under a new ID but with an
identical list of post texts. An ID-only check would accept it as a new example.
The local follow-up implementation instead treats it as an exact alias, alongside
accounts sharing an ID.

**Exact aliases stay together during splitting, and incompatible predefined
boundaries fail before fitting.** Generated outer holdout and out-of-fold splits
keep alias groups intact. If an alias crosses fixed official-shard, train/practice
or primary/auxiliary boundaries, the workflow stops rather than quietly moving or
dropping records. Conflicting labels within an alias group also stop the run. The
shipped overlapping official/practice pairing therefore cannot pass the current
optimisation guard unchanged.

The follow-up audit records input and label checksums, overlap counts and label
conflicts without publishing account records. These local safeguards postdate the
pinned historical run and tests above; they do not turn its saved practice results
into an independent assessment.

### Evaluating on independent accounts

The remaining assessment needs to happen in this order:

1. **Freeze the inputs and grouping rules.** Record checksums, label provenance and
   source/entity groups, with a declared near-duplicate policy. Exact matching
   misses paraphrases, edited timelines and other near duplicates.
2. **Reserve assessment data before selection.** Use independently labelled
   accounts with appropriate separation from development data, keeping their
   results out of model, ensemble-weight and threshold decisions.
3. **Fit within the permitted training partitions.** Learn TF-IDF vocabularies,
   SVD projections, scaling, calibration and models only from allowed training
   accounts. Internal probability calibration currently uses ungrouped folds;
   aliases can cross those internal folds, affecting the probabilities used for
   threshold selection. The protected outer boundaries still apply, but making
   calibration itself group-aware remains a separate modelling follow-up.
4. **Assess the fixed system on the reserved accounts.** Record per-language
   confusion counts, utility and F1 with the frozen model and threshold choices,
   without using those results to promote another candidate.

That assessment has not been run. The result I can currently defend is a
model-comparison pipeline with recorded decisions and increasingly careful
data-splitting safeguards; independent predictive performance remains unresolved.
