For the McGill Bot or Not challenge, I worked on a language-specific classifier
comparison and reporting pipeline. It combines account behavior, profile features,
and text representations to classify accounts. English and French use separate
models and thresholds; the bilingual wrapper coordinates their runs rather than
training one multilingual model.

## Comparing models before selecting a threshold

The pipeline compares logistic regression, a hybrid text model, histogram gradient
boosting, ExtraTrees, and an ensemble. The official-first workflow groups validation
by whole official dataset shards. Out-of-fold predictions select the model and
language-specific threshold, after which the estimator is refit on the combined
official training data. The run saves its configuration, selection report, model
bundle, and prediction artifacts.

This path differs from the lower-level training command, which can reserve a
stratified primary holdout. The saved official-first run has no additional primary
holdout (`selected_holdout` is null). The
[modeling tests](https://github.com/TheTexta/bot-or-not-mcgill/blob/5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4/tests/test_modeling.py)
check that reserved holdout accounts stay out of selection-time fitting and that
predefined official fold IDs are respected. Those tests protect split behavior;
the default mocked suite does not reproduce expensive numerical training results.

## What the saved practice scores mean

The run `20260404T162558Z-both-official-first-optimize` reports:

- [English practice shard 30](https://github.com/TheTexta/bot-or-not-mcgill/blob/5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4/runs/20260404T162558Z-both-official-first-optimize/en/practice-evaluation-report.json): F1 0.95238 across 275 accounts, with TP 60, FP 0, FN 6, and TN 209.
- [French practice shard 31](https://github.com/TheTexta/bot-or-not-mcgill/blob/5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4/runs/20260404T162558Z-both-official-first-optimize/fr/practice-evaluation-report.json): F1 1.000 across 171 accounts, with TP 27, FP 0, FN 0, and TN 144.

**These are saved practice results, not an independent unseen-content evaluation.**
An October 2026 audit of the checked-in inputs found exact complete-text-timeline
matches for 252 of 275 English practice accounts against official shards 1, 3,
and 5, and for 164 of 171 French practice accounts against official shard 2 alone.
English profiles differed: those 252 matches were not identical full records.
For French, 131 of 171 matched the full profile and timestamped-post payload after
excluding IDs. Account-ID disjointness alone does not catch renamed accounts with
reused content.

The audit matched SHA-256 fingerprints of sorted complete post-text lists, and
separately of canonical profile/post payloads with identifier fields excluded.
It applies to inputs at revision `5539649f4757570eab0c1bdc8d304597492d7aa4`.
The historical reports name the same dataset IDs but do not establish input-byte
parity through saved checksums. The training run and numerical scores were not
reproduced by that audit. The overlap limits the generalization claim; it does not
reconstruct every detail of the historical run or establish failure of the grouped
official validation.

## The next evaluation boundary

The useful engineering result is a repeatable model-comparison, selection, and
prediction workflow. The follow-up split audit records input and label checksums,
multiset account-ID and content overlap, and label conflicts without publishing
account records. Exact aliases stay together in outer holdout and out-of-fold
splits. Incompatible fixed-shard or train/practice boundaries fail before fitting
rather than silently moving records. The overlapping official/practice pairing
therefore cannot pass the current optimization guard unchanged.

These exact-match checks do not detect paraphrases or establish independence for
near duplicates. Internal estimator calibration is not group-aware and is not
presented as an additional independent assessment. A stronger generalization
result still requires a frozen protocol, independently labelled or appropriately
grouped assessment data, and evaluation after model and threshold choices are
fixed. That assessment remains future work. The historical practice artifacts
retain their original labels and should be read with the overlap context above.
